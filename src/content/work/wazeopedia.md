## The business

Wazeopedia is a knowledge hub created by Waze and maintained by its editors community. It holds editing guidelines for every community and general information about the Waze map and products.

## The problem

Users trusted Wazeopedia as the official source, but found it outdated and inconvenient. So many of them asked in forums, on Facebook or turned to friends instead.

## Who we talked to

We built three editor profiles from our research: a tour guide who edits maps at night, a regional council member who updates the map after road changes, and a startup worker who taught himself the editor and usually asks friends rather than the wiki.

## Key decisions

**Search first.** People arrive with a specific question, so search is the first thing they see. A dropdown with recent and recommended topics helps people who don't like typing.

**Forgiving search.** It accepts loose phrasing and suggests results based on keywords and past searches.

**A learning path.** Subject pages are split into clear topics that move from basic to advanced.

**Community identity.** The home page makes it clear this is a resource built by the community, for the community.

**Familiar visual language.** Users already know Waze, so we reused its look and feel.

## The app, before and after

Before, reaching the map editor meant a Google search and a lot of clicks. After, the editor lives in a dedicated area inside the app. We removed the extra registration step since users are already logged in, added a drawer with search, and kept the desktop pattern of showing favorites before any typing.
