## The challenge

I wanted to go beyond a record of what happened. The goal was to let people feel the instability of moving between the present and a memory that still feels immediate.

## Where the idea came from

During EMDR therapy I met bilateral stimulation, a back and forth movement that shaped both the interaction and the visual language of the project.

The second spark was a flickering screen that looked broken but kept transmitting. That became the core metaphor: **a system that still functions while something inside it remains disrupted.**

So human memory became computer memory. REM became RAM.

## Designing under constraints

I couldn't show real maps, places or operational information. Instead of fighting that, I turned it into the method. Maps and images are translated into ASCII characters and fragments. Each place keeps its emotional structure, without documentary realism.

## How it behaves

- Every memory is tied to a location and a recorded sound.
- When the sound plays, the interface breaks: maps fragment, text destabilizes, present day photos are interrupted by images from my service.
- When the sound ends, everything settles into a calm, static state.

That tension between a quiet present and a past that can come back without warning is the whole experience.

## Process

1. **Research.** My own therapy experience, and how traumatic memories are triggered and recalled.
2. **Concept.** The broken screen metaphor and the REM to RAM framework.
3. **Constraints.** ASCII abstraction to protect sensitive information.
4. **Interaction design.** Sound triggered disruption and the return to stillness.
5. **Visual language.** Glitch, distortion, broken pixels and desaturated, old film imagery.
6. **Build.** Front-end development with Claude Code, deployed on Vercel.

## Outcome

The project holds four personal memories today, but the structure supports more locations, recordings and testimonies. One direction I'd love to explore is a shared platform where other veterans can describe experiences that are hard to put into words.
