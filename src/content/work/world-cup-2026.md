## The context

ynet Sports needed an interactive match schedule for the 2026 World Cup. The tournament has a complex, multi stage structure, and the page had to make that whole structure easy to grasp.

## The problem

Classic schedules follow one fixed hierarchy, usually the date. If you only remember a team, a group or a stadium, you end up scanning long lists, switching filters or rebuilding details from memory. The interface makes the user adapt to the data, instead of the other way around.

## Visual research

I looked at tournament brackets, game interfaces, data tables and cross reference systems. Three references shaped the direction: FIFA's official 2026 site, MARCA's games board from the Qatar World Cup, and the Scrabble board.

## Key decisions

**Multiple entry points.** Date, team, tournament stage and stadium are all starting points. You begin from whatever you remember.

**Recognition over recall.** Every option stays visible, so nobody has to formulate a search query.

**Respond to movement.** Hovering over any data point highlights the related matches and pushes everything else back. It's instant and fully reversible.

**A playful data experience.** Borrowing from game boards and scoreboards, connected elements light up. Exploring feels fun without losing clarity.

## Outcome

The schedule turned from a static list into a system that supports both quick, goal driven lookups and wider exploration of the tournament.
